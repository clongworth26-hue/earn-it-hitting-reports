The FarmHand

If a kid did it on a farm fifty years ago, it belongs in this program.

If the exercise looks like something a kid would have done working on a farm 50 years ago, it's probably fine.

That is the whole filter. Not what looks good on Instagram. Not what a machine isolates. If it was a job — bale, bucket, fence post, wheelbarrow — it built a body that could do work. That is what we are rebuilding.

01

Five Rules Before Anything Else

RULE 01

Technique Before Load

Nothing gets heavier until rep ten looks like rep one. If the shape breaks, the set is over.

RULE 02

No Failure Sets

We stop at the last good rep, not the last possible one. Young bodies adapt to quality, not exhaustion.

RULE 03

No Max Testing

We never test a one-rep max on a youth athlete. We test the rep. The number is not the point.

RULE 04

Load By Implement

Hands, then a rock, then a sandbag, then a full bucket. The farm never had a weight room.

RULE 05

Every Rep Has A Job

If you cannot name what a movement is training, it comes out of the program.

05

The Eight Weeks

WEEKS 1–2
Learn

Level 1. Two sets instead of four. Nobody cares how much. Everybody cares how it looks.

WEEKS 3–4
Build

Level 2. Add the sets back. Same tempo every rep — no racing the clock.

WEEKS 5–6
Load

Level 3. The bag gets heavier. The shape does not change.

WEEK 7
Own It

Level 4, fewer reps. Crisp and heavy. This is the strongest week and it should feel like it.

WEEK 8
Reset

Back to Level 2. Re-test the movements by eye, not by number. Then start over heavier.

06

For The Parent Or Coach In The Room

Read this before week one

  • Soreness is fine. Joint pain is a stop. Muscle soreness two days later is normal. Sharp pain during a rep is not — change the movement, don't push through it.
  • During a growth spurt, drop the load and keep the pattern. Long bones outrun tendons. Coordination temporarily gets worse. That is normal, and it is not a reason to stop — it is a reason to go lighter.
  • Sleep and food are part of the program. A kid sleeping six hours will not adapt to this no matter how good the sets look.
  • Compare the athlete to themselves last month. Never to the 17-year-old in the next rack. That comparison has ended more youth careers than any injury.
  • Any movement can be regressed. If the sandbag is too much, it's a rock. If the rock is too much, it's hands. There is no shame in the ladder, only in skipping it.
07

What This Program Is Not

Not a max-out program. We never chase a number with a young spine.
Not machines. A leg extension never loaded a wagon.
Not a boot camp. No timed circuits to exhaustion.
Not a shortcut. Eight weeks is one block, not a body.
Not a competition. The only scoreboard is last month's you.
Not optional. The work shows up, or the results don't.
