6. Blowout Protocol — the general rule

IF WE ARE UP BIG (8+ after 4 innings, or 10+ anytime)
  • Starters come out at their planned cap. Never one pitch over to "save the bullpen."
  • Low-end arms eat the rest: Aymes, Ray, and — Saturday only — Peyton or Ro.
  • Top four (Marsh, Eli, Colton, Jase) stop. Their Sunday availability is the asset.
  • Never let a blowout turn a ≤20 outing into a 40-pitch outing.
  • Up big on Sunday: Aymes finishes, Jase sits.
IF THEY ARE UP BIG (down 8+ after 4, or 10+ anytime)
  • Chad's lean: low-end guys get short work. Don't spend a real arm on a lost game.
  • Pull the starter at ≤20 if he's near it — that's a free Sunday-legal arm.
  • Finish with Aymes + Ray (Friday/Saturday) or Aymes (Sunday).
  • Do not pitch Jase or Eli in a lost Saturday game. Their Sunday role outranks garbage innings.
  • Keep every low-end stint ≤20 so they stay flexible for the next day.
The principle: a blowout is the only time we get to give the bottom of the staff real innings. Use it. A 12-run game is where Aymes and Ray earn their tournament — not in a one-run seventh.

9. Before First Pitch — Confirm

  • Tournament daily cap. Assumed 95. Some events use 85 or a per-day team total.
  • Game length. Assumes 7 innings. If 6, every budget gets ~15 pitches of slack.
  • Re-entry rule. Can a pitcher return the same day after removal? Plan assumes no.
  • Three-day rule is ours, not theirs. Pitch Smart doesn't forbid it — this is a team development decision. If you want to override it during a game, say so now, not in the dugout.
  • Sunday single game. Confirmed. If a second Sunday game appears, this plan needs a re-cut.
  • Jase's Sunday leash. Plan gives him 25. If he's rolling, he can go longer — up to 95 — but then Colton and Marsh are done.
