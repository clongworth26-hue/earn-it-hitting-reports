01

Program Intent

This is the operating document behind the parent handout. Where the handout says "hay bale deadlift," this says what load, which cue, what fault you will actually see, and when to stop the set. Everything here is decided by eye and by rep quality — never by a number on a plate.

What we are building

General physical preparedness: hinge, squat, push, pull, carry, rotate, and locomotion. Enough strength to survive a growth spurt and enough trunk control to hold a throwing pattern together.

What we are not building

Max lifts. Sport-specific power. Body composition change. If a parent expects a max in eight weeks, correct the expectation on day one, not week eight.

The non-negotiable

Rep quality is the load. If the shape degrades, the set is over regardless of the number left on the card. No exceptions for a good athlete having a good day.

02

Session Structure

45–55 minutes. The floor clock runs the session, not the athlete's mood. If you are short on time, cut a set from the middle movement — never cut the warm-up or the cooldown.

BlockTimeWhat happensCoach priority
Chores (warm-up)8 minSkip/jog, hinge drill, squat hold, band workEyes on every athlete. This is where you spot the kid who is stiff today.
Field work26–32 min3–4 loaded movementsTwo cues per movement, maximum. More than two is noise.
Evening chores8 minCarries, sled, or the conditioning piecePosture under fatigue. This is the real test of the session.
Cooldown5 minWalk, breathe, hips and latsDown-regulate. Do not let them leave jacked up.
03

Load Assignment Rules

Loads are assigned by the rep, not by the calendar. These are starting points to be adjusted on the first set of every session — a growing 13-year-old is a different athlete in November than in September.

Movement familyStarting pointAdjust rule
Hinge (hay bale)Sandbag or rockLoad that allows 6 crisp reps with 2 genuinely in reserve. If rep 6 slows visibly or the back rounds, drop one implement level on the next set.
Squat (stone pick)Ball at chestStart near 10% of bodyweight; add only while the heels stay down and the chest stays tall through rep 8.
Carry (bucket line)Two bucketsTotal load near 20–30% of bodyweight. The test is that they can walk 30 yd without leaning away from the load.
Sled (wheelbarrow)Bodyweight or lessThey should be able to push 20 yd without stopping. If they stall, lighten the sled, not the distance.
Sprints (cow chase)BodyweightNever timed for a score. Full walk-back recovery. If form falls apart, the set is over.
Throws (axe swing)6–10 lb ballEffort is high, volume is low. 5 reps per side. Stop the moment the rotation gets sloppy.

Rep Quality Scale — use this instead of a rep count

  • 3 — Owned. Rep 6 looks like rep 1. Speed did not drop. Load can go up next week.
  • 2 — Held. Slight slowdown, shape intact. Stay at this load one more week.
  • 1 — Broke. Shape changed at any point. Set ends immediately, load drops one level.

Entry Screen & Testing

Screen every athlete before week one. Pass/coach, not pass/fail — a coachable marker is where you start, not a reason to turn a kid away. Never turn a screen into a score they can lose.

ScreenWhat you're watchingCoachable markerWhere to start
Overhead squat, dowelHeels, depth, arms overheadHeels lift or arms come forwardLevel 1 squat, hold + ankle rocks
Dowel hip hingeSpine shape through the hingeBack rounds earlyLevel 1 hinge, hands to knees
Overhead reach, wallRibs, low back, shoulder rangeArching to get overheadBand dislocates, shorter range press
Single-leg balance, 10sFoot, knee, hip controlKnee caves, foot rollsSplit squat with dowel before loaded
Push-up, 5 repsBody line, shoulder positionHips sag or head leadsIncline push-up, plank hold

Testing protocol — week 8

  • No 1-rep max testing, ever, at any age in this program. It is not the metric and it is not worth the risk.
  • Re-screen the five movements. Compare the athlete to their own week-one video, not to the athlete next to them.
  • Record the implement level and the rep quality score for each main lift. That is the progress report.
  • Video is the artifact. Phone on a tripod, side angle, same distance each block. Opinions are not data.
08

Progression Decision Rules

SituationAction
Two weeks at rep quality 3 on all main liftsAdd one implement level to the hinge and squat. Keep carries the same for one more week.
Rep quality 2 for two weeksHold the load. This is normal and it is where adaptation actually happens. Do not rush it.
Any rep quality 1Drop one level immediately, finish the set. Reassess next session.
Visible growth spurt — clumsy, longer limbsDrop one implement level for 2–3 weeks. Keep every pattern. This is maintenance, not regression.
Athlete missed a weekRestart the ladder at Level 1 for one session. No exceptions, no catching up.
Athlete in-season, high game volumeTwo days per week. Cut sprint volume first, then the finisher carries.
09

Stop Rules

End the session and notify the parent

  1. Sharp or pinpoint joint pain at any load, including bodyweight.
  2. Any pain that changes how they move — limping, guarding, refusing the position.
  3. Low-back pain that travels into the hip, glute, or leg.
  4. Any pop, snap, or give-way in a joint. No "walk it off."
  5. Dizziness, headache, or vision change — heat, hydration, or worse. Sit them down.
  6. An athlete hiding or downplaying pain. That is a stop on its own; the injury is secondary.
10

Coach Language

Say this

  • "Same shape as rep one."
  • "Leave two in the tank."
  • "That's the set — good work."
  • "Show me the box, not the load."
  • "How did that feel compared to last week?"

Never say this

  • "Push through it."
  • "One more rep."
  • "No pain, no gain."
  • "You're not trying hard enough."
  • Any comparison to another athlete, out loud.

Floor note

  • Public comparison is the fastest way to lose a 13-year-old. Praise the rep, never the ranking.
  • If a parent asks for a max number, show them the video instead. That is the actual evidence of progress.
  • If you are unsure about a load, go lighter. There is no prize for being wrong heavier.
