What These Numbers Mean  (tap to expand)

Tap any metric for a plain-English explanation of what it means on the field.

Average Exit Velocity ▼

Plain English: How hard you hit the ball, on average

On the field: Higher exit velocity = ball comes off the bat faster = harder for defense to handle. Hitting 45+ mph at 9U means infielders have less time to react.

Focus: 50+ mph consistently

Peak Exit Velocity ▼

Plain English: Your hardest-hit ball

On the field: Shows your top-end power potential. The harder you CAN hit it, the more damage you can do when you barrel one up.

Focus: 60+ mph

Peak-8 Average ▼

Plain English: Average of your 8 hardest hits

On the field: More reliable than a single peak. If your top 8 are all around the same speed, you're consistently finding the barrel.

Focus: tighter spread = more consistent

Average Launch Angle ▼

Plain English: The angle the ball leaves your bat

On the field: Too low (<5°) = ground balls (easy outs). Too high (>30°) = popups (also outs). The sweet spot (10-25°) produces line drives and hard-hit fly balls.

Focus: 10-25° sweet spot

Hard Hit Percentage ▼

Plain English: % of swings at 75+ mph

On the field: Balls hit 75+ mph are much harder to field. At 9U, getting to 20%+ means you're consistently hitting with authority.

Focus: 20%+

Barrel Percentage ▼

Plain English: % of swings with max exit velo + ideal launch angle

On the field: Barrels are the perfect combination of speed and launch — these are doubles, triples, and home runs. Even 5-10% is excellent for this age.

Focus: 5%+

Average Distance ▼

Plain English: How far your balls travel, on average

On the field: More distance = more gaps, more extra-base hits. At 9U, 80+ ft average means you're driving the ball through the infield consistently.

Focus: 80+ ft

Max Distance ▼

Plain English: Your farthest-hit ball

On the field: Shows power ceiling. The longer your max, the more home run potential as you grow.

Focus: 120+ ft

pBA — Protected Batting Average ▼

Plain English: Your expected batting average based on exit velocity and contact quality

On the field: Unlike regular batting average (which depends on fielders, luck, and opponent), pBA measures ONLY what you control — exit velocity and contact quality. A .200 pBA means a player at your level who hits the ball this hard would be expected to get a hit 20% of the time.

Level average varies by age group

Whiff Rate ▼

Plain English: How often you swing and miss

On the field: Every miss is a guaranteed out. Reducing whiffs means more balls in play = more chances to get on base.

Focus: under 20%
